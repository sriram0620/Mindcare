Dissociative Disorders

"Don't call it dissociative identity disorder, Call it backup personalities."

Dissociative disorders are mental health conditions that involve feelings of being detached from reality, being outside of your own body, or experiencing memory loss (amnesia). The word "dissociation" means to be disconnected from others, from the world around you, or from yourself.

Types of Dissociative Disorders

Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID)

People with DID have two or more separate identities. These identities control their behavior at various times.

Dissociative Amnesia

This condition happens when you can't remember essential information about your life.

Depersonalization Disorder

A condition where you feel detached from your thoughts, feelings, and body.

Common Symptoms

DID Symptoms

  • Multiple distinct identities
  • Memory gaps
  • Sudden behavioral changes
  • Identity confusion

Amnesia Symptoms

  • Memory loss of specific events
  • Confusion about identity
  • Depression or anxiety
  • Relationship difficulties

Medication

  • Anti-anxiety medication
  • Mood stabilizers
  • Sleep aids

  • Community resources
