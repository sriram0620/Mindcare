Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)

"The mind is a powerful thing. It can take you to a place of peace or a place of chaos."

What is Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder?

Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) is a mental health condition characterized by persistent, unwanted thoughts (obsessions) and repetitive behaviors (compulsions). These obsessions and compulsions can significantly impact daily life and functioning.

People with OCD often feel compelled to perform certain rituals or routines to alleviate the anxiety caused by their obsessions, but these compulsions provide only temporary relief.

Symptoms of OCD

Common Obsessions

  • Fear of contamination or dirt
  • Fear of harming oneself or others
  • Need for order or symmetry
  • Intrusive thoughts about taboo subjects

Common Compulsions

  • Excessive cleaning or handwashing
  • Checking locks, appliances repeatedly
  • Counting or repeating actions
  • Arranging items in a particular order

Causes of OCD

Biological Factors

Differences in brain structure and function may play a role in OCD development.

Genetic Factors

OCD tends to run in families, suggesting a genetic component.

Environmental Factors

Stressful life events, trauma, and infections may trigger OCD.

Treatment Options

Psychotherapy

  • Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)
  • Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP)
  • Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)

Medications

  • Fluoxetine
  • Fluvoxamine
  • Paroxetine
  • Sertraline

Self-Care

  • Regular exercise
  • Stress management
  • Support groups
  • Mindfulness practices
